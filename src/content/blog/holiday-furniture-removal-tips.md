The new sectional is ordered. The delivery window is set. Then somebody asks, "So where's the old couch going?"

This time of year we hear that question a lot across St. Louis and St. Charles County. New furniture comes in for the holidays, and the old couch, bed frame or dining set has to go somewhere. Here's how to sort it out before the delivery truck pulls up.

## Start with a plan before delivery day

The worst spot to be in is a new couch on the porch and the old one still in the living room. Take ten minutes this week and decide:

- **What's leaving?** Couch, loveseat, mattress and box spring, bed frame, table and chairs, a dresser that no longer fits.
- **What shape is it in?** Be honest. Clean and sturdy, or torn, stained and sagging?
- **Where is it?** Ground floor near the door is easy. A basement or second floor with tight stairs is a different job.

Those three answers usually tell you which option below makes sense.

## Option 1: Curbside bulk pickup

Many cities and trash haulers around St. Louis County and St. Charles County offer some kind of bulk item pickup. It can be the cheapest route, but the rules vary a lot from one city to the next.

### What to check first

- **Do you have to schedule it?** Many programs want a call or online request ahead of time.
- **How many items?** Some limit how much can go out at once.
- **Which day?** Bulk pickup isn't always your regular trash day.
- **Any special rules?** Mattresses, box springs and items with Freon often have their own rules.

Call your city or check your trash hauler's website. Don't drag a couch to the curb and hope. A sofa sitting in the rain for a week is no fun for you or your neighbors, and some areas will cite you for it.

## Option 2: Donate it

If the old piece is still in good shape, someone else may be glad to have it. Before you load it up, keep a few things in mind.

### What donation spots usually look for

- **Clean, no rips, no stains.** Upholstered pieces get turned down most often.
- **No pet hair or smoke smell.** These are common reasons for a no.
- **All the parts.** Bed frames with their hardware, tables with their leaves, chairs that don't wobble.
- **Mattresses are often a no.** Many places won't take them at all.

Call ahead and ask what they take right now. Some offer pickup, but those slots can fill up weeks out around the holidays. If you're hoping to have it gone before guests arrive, plan for that.

You can also offer it free to a neighbor, a family member or a local online group. Just be ready for people who say they'll come and never show.

## Option 3: Ask the furniture store

Some retailers will take the old piece when they bring the new one, sometimes for a fee. Ask when you schedule delivery. Find out what it costs, what they'll take and whether it has to be ready by the door. This works best for a one-for-one swap, like an old couch for a new couch.

## When hauling it makes sense

Sometimes the easy answer is to have it carried out. That's usually the case when:

- **It's heavy or awkward.** Sleeper sofas, solid wood dressers, big sectionals.
- **It's in a tough spot.** A basement, a second floor or a tight hallway nobody wants to fight with.
- **There's more than one piece.** The couch, plus the old recliner, plus the bed set from the guest room.
- **Timing matters.** You need it gone before delivery or before family shows up.
- **It's not worth donating.** Worn out, broken or stained.
